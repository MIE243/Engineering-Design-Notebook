## Summary
Developed CD2 as a concept for a teaching drivetrain model mounted on two independently driven belts. The belts would impose different left and right wheel speeds to represent a turning condition and make differential behaviour observable.

## Sketch and concept development
![[2026-10-04 CD2 Concept Sketch.png]]

The sketch explores the belt platform and drivetrain as separate parts, followed by a combined concept illustration. The platform includes two belts, electric belt drives, a control panel, and a stop/restraint to keep the model positioned on the platform. The drivetrain sketch includes wheels, axle differentials, and a transfer case with a selector lever.

The lower illustration communicates a possible arrangement with a transparent drivetrain enclosure, separate belt-speed controls, and a buffer stop. It is a concept visualization, not a completed CAD design or evidence of tested performance.

## Intended demonstration
- Students adjust the belt speeds to create a difference between the left and right wheel speeds.
- The wheels contact the belts, while the restraint keeps the drivetrain model in place.
- Students compare wheel and differential motion to investigate how the drivetrain accommodates the imposed speed difference.

## Design considerations and next steps
- Determine how the belt drives and drivetrain input will interact; the sketch does not yet establish the complete power flow.
- Verify adequate wheel-to-belt traction and model restraint without obstructing the mechanism.
- Define belt-speed control, wheel-speed limits, and visible markers for observation.
- Consider guarding, pinch points, and accessible stopping controls around the belts and gears.
- Evaluate platform size, motor requirements, cost, and whether the road-simulation base fits the agreed project scope.
